# AIRS Example Research Reports

This directory contains real research sessions generated by AIRS across
public companies, cryptocurrencies, and private/startup companies.

These reports are preserved as historical research snapshots. Each report
represents the evidence available to AIRS at the time the research session
was generated, along with the resulting directional hypothesis and
uncertainty assessment.

## File Naming

The reports in this directory use simplified filenames such as `AAPL.md`,
`Bitcoin.md`, and `Anthropic.md` to make the examples easier to browse.

The original AIRS-generated reports are stored in `reports/output/` using
timestamped filenames that identify the research session, for example:

`Bitcoin_20260814_064833.md`

The `reports/output/` directory contains runtime-generated reports and is
excluded from version control. The files in this directory are curated
examples preserved for documentation, demonstration, and historical
evaluation purposes.

The simplified filenames do not represent a change to the underlying AIRS
report format.

## Why These Reports Are Preserved

AIRS is designed to evaluate its research quality over time.

A research session is not treated as a permanently correct prediction.
Instead, the original research output is preserved as a historical
checkpoint and can later be evaluated against subsequent outcomes.

The evaluation process follows this general lifecycle:

```text
Research Session
      ↓
Evidence + Analysis
      ↓
Directional Hypothesis
      ↓
Historical Snapshot
      ↓
Evaluation Period
      ↓
Actual Outcome
      ↓
Audit / Evaluation
      ↓
Reward or Penalty
```

For example, a research session generated for a company can be preserved
and evaluated approximately 30 days later using information that became
available after the original research session.

This allows AIRS to measure whether its historical research hypotheses were
supported or contradicted by subsequent outcomes rather than judging the
research only at the time it was generated.

## Adaptive Research

Research depth varies by entity. AIRS first probes available research
dimensions and then adaptively determines how much research is required
based on evidence availability and consistency.

A publicly traded company may have access to quantitative, technical, and
business evidence and therefore require multiple research iterations.

A private company or startup may have limited quantitative data and may
therefore complete with fewer research iterations.

The examples in this directory demonstrate this difference.

## Historical Evaluation & 30-Day Audit

AIRS preserves completed research sessions as historical snapshots that can
later be evaluated against actual market outcomes.

For assets with a ticker, AIRS uses a fixed 30-day evaluation horizon.
The audit process retrieves the relevant price data, calculates the
30-day percentage change, and evaluates the original directional bias
using a deterministic graded score ranging from `-1.0` to `+1.0`.

The evaluation flow is:

```text
Research Session
      ↓
Evidence Snapshot
      ↓
Directional Bias + Uncertainty
      ↓
Historical Session Stored
      ↓
30-Day Evaluation Period
      ↓
Actual Price Outcome
      ↓
Deterministic Outcome Scoring
      ↓
Accuracy Score (-1.0 → +1.0)
```

A positive score indicates that the subsequent price movement supported the
original directional hypothesis, while a negative score indicates that the
outcome moved against it.

The scoring system is graded rather than binary. AIRS does not simply mark
a research session as "correct" or "incorrect"; the magnitude of the
subsequent price movement affects the resulting score.

Neutral hypotheses are also evaluated. A neutral hypothesis receives a
positive score when the subsequent movement remains within the defined
neutral range and can receive a penalty when the asset experiences a
significant movement.

## Historical Integrity

The original research session is preserved before the evaluation takes
place. This allows AIRS to evaluate the research using information that
became available after the original session rather than modifying the
original analysis retroactively.

The reports in this directory therefore serve two purposes:

- Examples — demonstrating the reports generated by AIRS across
different asset types.

- Historical Research Records — preserving representative research
outputs that can be used alongside the audit infrastructure for
evaluating research quality over time.

## Asset Types

Public companies and cryptocurrencies with tickers can receive 30-day
price-based outcome scores.

Private companies and startups without market tickers are still analyzed
by AIRS, but they are not assigned a price-based 30-day outcome score.

Future versions may introduce alternative outcome proxies for private
companies and startups.
